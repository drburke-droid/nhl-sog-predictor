a single game, race, jump or throw, compared with the best single day by anyone else in the 15 seasons either side.

**Description**

```
Wilt Chamberlain's 100 points. Kobe Bryant's 81. Bob Beamon's 8.90 m. Which single day stood furthest above everyone else of its era?

We took the best single game, race, jump or throw of every season in 59 categories and compared each one with the best single day by anyone else in the 15 seasons before and after, around a curved trend. For team sports we also allowed for the league's scoring level. The score is how many "usual gaps" a performance sits above its era: grading on a curve.

The countdown:
10. Johannes Vetter, 2020: 97.76 m javelin
9. Léon Marchand, 2025: 1:52.69, 200 m individual medley
8. Sergey Bubka, 1993: 6.15 m pole vault (indoors)
7. Brian Oldfield, 1975: 22.86 m shot put (a professional mark, never ratified)
6. Yulimar Rojas, 2021: 15.67 m triple jump
5. Katie Ledecky, 2016: 8:04.79, 800 m freestyle
4. Kobe Bryant, 2005–06: 81 points in a game
3. Jan Železný, 1996: 98.48 m javelin
2. Wilt Chamberlain, 1961–62: 100 points in a game
1. Bob Beamon, 1968: 8.90 m long jump

Why hockey and home runs aren't here: they come in small whole numbers. In most seasons everyone else's best night is the same 7 or 8 points, or 3 home runs, so one more looks enormous or like nothing. Darryl Sittler's 10-point game scores 4.3 and ranks 16th; we show why. The ranking keeps stats that come in big numbers: points, rebounds, assists, strikeouts, yards, and every athletics and swimming mark.

What this is not: a ranking of the greatest athletes, or a probability. The top four (Beamon, Chamberlain, Železný, Bryant) were in the top ten under at least 15 of 16 versions of the method; places 5 to 10 change with small choices. Léon Marchand's 2025 has only past seasons to compare with.

Data notes: NBA box scores before 1955–56 are incomplete, and NFL games before 1999 aren't openly available, so those seasons are left out. Single games include overtime and extra innings.
```
