## Description

```
What if your robot vacuum moved at the speed of light? This one cleans every floor of every building in New York, all 1,081,950 of them, and then every floor on Earth.

🧹 The Empire State Building (261,312 m², 102 floors): 3.5 milliseconds.
🧹 Every floor in New York (535 million m²): 7.4 seconds. That's 2.1 million km of rows, 5.6 times the distance to the Moon.
🧹 Every floor on Earth (about 273 billion m², about 2.5 billion buildings): about 1 hour 8 minutes. The rows, end to end, reach farther than Jupiter is from the Sun.
🧹 The dustbin: about 82,000 tonnes of dust, half a billion bin-fulls, 173 years of emptying.
🧹 The catch: at 99% of light speed, every U-turn at the end of a row releases about 10 Tsar Bombas. It makes about 273 billion of them.

Where the world's floor area comes from: UN Environment Programme & the Global Alliance for Buildings and Construction, Global Status Report for Buildings and Construction 2025–2026: 273 billion m² of building floor area in 2024.

It's the sequel to "How long would a Roomba take to drive every road in America at the speed of light?" That Roomba had no map and drove at random. This robot has a map: rows 25 cm apart, back and forth, never going back.

What should it clean next? Tell me in the comments.
```
