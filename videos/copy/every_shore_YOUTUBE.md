## Description

A fish of light sets off down the Bow River in Calgary with one rule: keep the land on your right.

At every fork it turns right. At every river's source it turns round and swims back down the other bank. Then it carries on to the next river, the next lake, the next coast, until it has swum every shore on Earth: every coastline, every lake shore, and both banks of every river in OpenStreetMap.

At the speed of light the whole journey takes 1 minute 18.4 seconds. The middle of the film runs in real time.

The numbers:
• Coastline: 1.95 million km
• Lake shores: 1.13 million km
• Riverbanks: 2 × 9.58 million km
• Short links across gaps in the river map: 0.41 million km
• Open-sea swims out to islands: 0.87 million km
• Total: 23.5 million km, or 78.4 light-seconds

The CIA World Factbook says the world's coastline is 356,000 km long. Traced at OpenStreetMap's detail it's 1.95 million km. Both are right: the closer you look, the longer a coastline gets. That's the coastline paradox.

How it was made: every coastline (835,314 of them, one per island and continent), every OpenStreetMap river line (2 million) and every lake over 5 km² went into one network. Keeping the land on your right is the classic way out of a maze, and on a map it traces 1.3 million closed loops, one per piece of land. The fish crosses between loops where a river separates them, and swims across open sea to islands as it passes them. River basins that no water connects to the sea (the Okavango, the Aral Sea, Lake Eyre, Lake Chad) and rivers with gaps in their mapping it reaches by a hypothetical leap.

This is a thought experiment: nothing swims at the speed of light, but the map, the rule and the arithmetic are real.
