```
Light could circle the Earth 7½ times in one second. To feel what that means, we raced 120 years of the fastest machines ever built down one desert road, each at its real top speed, from the Wright Flyer to the Parker Solar Probe. Then we sent a pulse of light round the planet to race them.

If you watched light drive every road on Earth, this is the same speed from the other end: the fastest things we have ever built, side by side with it.

⚡ Wright Flyer, 1903: 30 mph. Light is 22 million times faster.
⚡ Spitfire 370 mph, Bell X-1 807 mph, F-35 1,228 mph, X-15 4,520 mph (still the fastest crewed, powered aircraft ever flown).
⚡ Parker Solar Probe, the fastest machine ever built: 430,000 mph. Light is still 1,560 times faster.
⚡ While the Wright Flyer crosses the picture (about 2.3 seconds), light goes round the Earth 17 times.
⚡ Parker crosses the whole picture in 1.25 thousandths of a second. Light crosses it in 1.3 millionths.
⚡ To the Moon: Wright Flyer 332 days, Spitfire 27 days, F-35 8 days, X-15 2.2 days, Parker 33 minutes. Light: 1.3 seconds.

"Time stopped" in the video means slow motion a million times slower, like a camera filming 25 million frames a second. Even then, light crosses the picture in about 35 frames.

How it's made: a real drone shot of a desert road (Pexels 11276661), with the aircraft rendered in Blender over it, lit and shadowed to match. Models: FlightGear's Wright Flyer, Spitfire IX, Bell X-1, F-35 and X-15 (GPL) and NASA's Parker Solar Probe (public domain). Every machine flies at its real top speed, 13 to 100 m from the camera, and the motion blur, sonic booms, shock waves and dust follow the physics at those speeds. Some of it is a thought experiment: the X-1 and F-35 fly at their record Mach numbers at sea level, and nothing has ever flown through air at Parker's speed. What you see is what the physics says it would do. The light pulse flies 250 m out, each lap 3 m higher than the last. The lines it leaves are markers so you can count the laps (real light leaves no trail), and light travels in straight lines, so imagine it steered round the equator.
Narration: AI voice (ElevenLabs).
Method, physics and code: https://github.com/drburke-droid/nhl-sog-predictor/blob/master/world_roads/speed_ladder/README.md
```
