## Thumbnails and titles

Each thumbnail has one big subject and two or three huge words. Each title gives the context; the thumbnail gives the
punch, and the two never repeat each other's words. Upload all three thumbnails to YouTube's Test & Compare: it keeps
the one that earns the most watch time. If your account can test titles too, pair them as in the table below.
Otherwise, use title A.

Why A leads:
* **It keeps its promise.** The F-35 is the first thing a viewer sees after clicking, so the opening seconds deliver
  exactly what the thumbnail showed. That holds viewers, and holding them is what YouTube rewards.
* **It reads instantly.** A fighter jet is recognisable at any size, and "TOO SLOW." under the fastest thing most
  people know sets up the question the video answers.
* **The title promises something to see.** It names a huge, exact number and something everyone recognises: "fighter
  jet" reaches people who wouldn't search "F-35". At 53 characters it shows in full on phones.

Other titles to try later: *The Speed of Light, Next to a Fighter Jet*, *Fighter Jet vs. the Speed of Light*, *What
the Speed of Light Actually Looks Like*, *How Fast Is Light? (Real Speeds, Real Physics)*.

How it's made: a real drone shot of a desert road (Pexels 11276661), with the aircraft rendered in Blender over it, lit and shadowed to match. Models: FlightGear's Wright Flyer, Spitfire IX, Bell X-1, F-35 and X-15 (GPL) and NASA's Parker Solar Probe (public domain). Every machine flies at its real top speed, 13 to 100 m from the camera, and the motion blur, sonic booms, shock waves and dust follow the physics at those speeds. Some of it is a thought experiment: the X-1 and F-35 fly at their record Mach numbers at sea level, and nothing has ever flown through air at Parker's speed. What you see is what the physics says it would do. The light pulse flies 250 m out, each lap 3 m higher than the last. ## Launch checklist

* **When:** Thursday at 12:00 pm Calgary time (2 pm Eastern, 7 pm UK), or about 2 hours before your audience's peak
  in YouTube Studio (Analytics, Audience, "When your viewers are on YouTube").
* **Upload a day early and schedule it,** so HD processing is done when it goes live.
* **At launch:** start the thumbnail test, pin the comment, add the end screen (World Roads and flights videos),
  and answer comments for the first hour.
* **Turn on "Altered or synthetic content".** The aircraft are CG over real footage, some of what they do never
  happened, and the voice is AI.
* **After 48 hours:** if click-through on impressions is low (under about 4%), try thumbnail B or C as the main one.
* **Shorts:** a vertical cut of the F-35 boom and its replay, or of light passing the frozen Parker, posted a day or
  two after the video and linking to it, sends new viewers to the full film.
* **GitHub link:** it works once `world_roads/speed_ladder` is merged to master. Until then, use the branch:
