**Description**

```
Ever wish you could show someone how blurry the world looks without your glasses? Your phone can do it. Hold your glasses over the camera, lock the focus, take the glasses away, and the camera sees the way you do without them.

How to do it (iPhone; Android is similar):
1. Open the camera and point it at something far away, 6 feet or more.
2. Hold your glasses in front of the camera lens, so the camera looks through them.
3. Press and hold the screen until AE/AF LOCK appears.
4. Take the glasses away. The focus stays locked.
5. Snap a photo: that's your uncorrected vision. Exit the camera app to unlock the focus.

Why it works: a nearsighted lens moves the camera's focus closer. Through a −3.00 lens, light from far away looks as if it starts about 33 cm (13 inches) away, so that's where the camera focuses. Lock it there and remove the lens, and the camera is nearsighted, just like you: things around 33 cm look sharp, and anything farther gets blurrier.

The catch: it only works for nearsighted (minus) prescriptions, roughly −0.50 to −10.00, and it can't show astigmatism. A camera can only blur evenly, so the cylinder ("cyl") part of a prescription won't show up.
```
