TITLE
-----
200,000 Years of Human Migration, Told in Sand

Alternatives (for a title test, or other platforms):
  200,000 Years of Human Migration in 3½ Minutes
  Every Human Alive Came From Here: 200,000 Years of Migration


DESCRIPTION
-----------
Every person alive descends from the same small groups of people in Africa. This is 200,000 years of human migration and mixing, shown as coloured sand in glass vessels shaped like the land.

HOW TO READ IT
• Each colour of sand is a strand of ancestry: the ancestry components used in ancient-DNA studies. They are labels for ancient populations, not races.
• Each glass vessel is a region, holding the average ancestry of the people who live there today.
• Sand is proportional to population, so the map starts as a sparse scatter and fills as humanity grows: 1 grain ≈ 120 people 200,000 years ago, ≈ 56,000 today.
• The mountains are the world's great cities (about 5 million people or more): the bigger the city, the higher the summit and the wider the base. Tokyo is the tallest. Coastal cities spill over the glass.
• White sand is Neanderthal ancestry; pale blue is Denisovan.

WHAT'S SIMPLIFIED
• Many dates are debated, so the film gives ranges or minimums: the main exit from Africa 70,000–50,000 years ago (earlier ventures, about 120,000 years ago, left little trace); Australia by at least 50,000 years ago (possibly 65,000); the Americas by at least 20,000 years ago (the White Sands footprints suggest 23,000–21,000).
• Regions show today's average ancestry, so colonised countries such as the US, Canada and Australia look mostly European. Indigenous peoples and their ancestry continue there.
• Ancestry is really continuous; the colours are broad, standard components. Population sizes before farming are rough estimates (within about 3×), and to keep the early map readable a grain stands for fewer people then than today.
• City mountains are drawn bigger than their share of the sand so they can be seen: a summit's height ranks cities by size (it grows a little slower than population, so Tokyo isn't a needle), and the mountains borrow sand from the surrounding countryside.
• City sizes are 2025 urban-agglomeration estimates (UN World Urbanization Prospects), rounded.
• Polynesia is drawn as Hawai'i but stands for Polynesians as a people, whose ancestors reached Tonga and Samoa about 2,900 years ago.
