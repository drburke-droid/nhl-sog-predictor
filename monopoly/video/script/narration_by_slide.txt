5. Decision 2: Trading (3:22)
======================================================================

[Slide 5.1 · 3:22–3:26]
ON SCREEN: Photo: THE CURB MARKET, WALL STREET, c. 1920 · Plaque: DECISION TWO / TO TRADE
NARRATION: Decision two. Trading.

[Slide 5.2 · 3:26–3:40]
ON SCREEN: The one that surprised me most. · My St. James for your Ventnor? · Not that trading helps. How much. · We both get a set. · REFUSED · Never trades · 5 · percent · A fifth of a fair share. · a typical player: 25%
NARRATION: This is the one that surprised me most. Not that trading helps. Everyone knows trading helps. It's how much it matters. A player who refuses every trade wins about five percent of games. Five. A fifth of a fair share. Almost nothing else in this video comes close.

[Slide 5.3 · 3:40–4:04]
ON SCREEN: My St. James for your Ventnor? · A set for a set. · REPLY: Deal. · ACCEPTED · any difference settled at the printed prices · Your set is better than mine. · Throw in $200 and · it’s a deal. · The haggler: · 21%, instead of 25% · They walk away. Nobody gets a set. · NO DEAL
NARRATION: So when the deal is on the table, a set for a set, take it. And if one side's cards cost more, settle the difference at the printed prices, and nothing more. Because the other way to ruin a trade is to haggle. "Your set is better than mine, so throw in some cash." A player who does that wins about twenty-one percent, instead of twenty-five. The other player walks away, and neither of you gets a set.

[Slide 5.4 · 4:04–4:12]
ON SCREEN: OTHER WAYS TO TRADE · Games won (%) · Typical: take every fair swap · 25.1 · Wait until you have the / cash to build · no difference · Hold out for the better set · 25.6 · barely
NARRATION: What about waiting until you've got the cash to build before trading? It makes no difference. Holding out for the better set? It barely helps.

[Slide 5.5 · 4:12–4:38]
ON SCREEN: THE ENDGAME: THE BOARD HAS LOCKED UP · I’ll buy your Kentucky Ave. · for $550 cash. · It finishes my reds. · NO! · REPLY: Yes. Deal. · Wins / first to go broke: · refuse .. 24.2% / 28.7% · sell .... 25.1% / 25.1% · TAKE THE · DEAL
NARRATION: And remember the endgame, when the board locks up and there's no fair swap left? Someone offers to buy the card that would finish their set, for two and a half times its printed price. Every instinct you have says no. Don't give them the set. Say yes. Players who refuse win a little less, and they're more likely to be the first one to go broke. The cash lets you build on what you've already got. In a stalled game, the deal is the move.
