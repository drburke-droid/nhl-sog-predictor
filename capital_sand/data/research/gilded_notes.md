# Largest private fortunes 1900–1986: research notes

All money is nominal USD millions. "Share" means fortune ÷ US nominal GDP in the same year (MeasuringWorth series below).
Files: `people.csv` (96 people), `anchors.csv` (178 anchors), `transfers.csv` (84 flows), `us_gdp_1900_1928.csv`.
The files were generated by `../build.py`, which holds every number with its source.

## GDP series
- `us_gdp_1900_1928.csv` comes from **MeasuringWorth** (Johnston & Williamson, "What Was the U.S. GDP Then?", nominal GDP).
  Its 1929 value is 104.556, which equals BEA's $104.6B. MeasuringWorth uses BEA NIPA from 1929 on, so the two join exactly.
- The splice cost nothing: 1928 = 98.3 and 1929 = 104.6 come from one continuous series. MeasuringWorth's pre-1929 values are its own compilation of historical estimates (see its sources page).
- Pre-1929 GDP is itself a reconstruction, but its uncertainty is small next to the uncertainty in the fortunes.
- The share calculations below use the same series to 1986 (e.g. 1957 = 474.0, 1968 = 940.7, 1982 = 3,343.8, 1986 = 4,579.6 $B).
- GBP fortunes were converted at MeasuringWorth's annual $/£ rate (1933: 4.24; 1936: 4.97).

## Kinds of anchors (read `kind` before using a number)
1. **Contemporary lists.** These are the most consistent cross-sections:
   - B.C. Forbes, Forbes, Mar 2 1918: 30 names, a bankers' consensus.
   - Fortune, Nov 1957: 76 names in brackets, so recorded as low/high.
   - Fortune, May 1968, "America's Centimillionaires": the online text gives only a few figures. The p.156 table is not online, so Getty and Hughes have no 1968 figure.
   - Forbes 400, 1982–86, via UPI wire reports and the Moretti–Wilson Forbes-400 panel.
2. **Estates at death** (probate or transfer-tax appraisals in the NYT and wire reports).
   - These often understate peak wealth because of lifetime gifts and trusts.
   - Examples: Rockefeller $26M, Phipps $3M, Payne $32M, Weyerhaeuser $0.9M.
3. **Later reconstructions.**
   - Mostly Klepper & Gunther, *The Wealthy 100* (1996). Their figures are tagged `Klepper & Gunther` in `kind`.
   - K&G figures are **not net worth at death**. They behave like a lifetime-fortune proxy that appears to add back gifts: Carnegie $475M vs a $30M estate; Rockefeller $1.4B vs a $26M estate; Eastman $95M vs mostly given away.
   - K&G's ratio uses GNP of the **death year**. Use them as a ceiling or for ordering, not as a dated point.
4. **Single-source or derived points.** Each is flagged in `kind`:
   - Edsel Ford 1943 = Time's "75% tax = $225M", so holdings ≈ $300M.
   - Ludwig ~1980: an obituary's undated "$3B" peak; the year is approximate.
   - Krupp 1962: Britannica's ">$1B", recorded as a lower bound.
   - W.W. Astor 1919: NYC real estate only, a lower bound.

**Biggest gap:** John D. Rockefeller Jr. in the 1920s.
- He received most of his father's fortune between 1917 and 1922. Over his life he gave away $537M and gave $240M to family.
- So his 1920s fortune was probably several hundred million: 1/178 if ~$500M.
- The commonly repeated "~$450–500M received" is **not verified** to a primary source, so no anchor was recorded for his 1920s fortune.

**Pre-window holders:** Alfred G. Vanderbilt held a ~$42M inheritance at the start of 1900 (1/505), but the anchor is dated 1899.

## Biggest uncertainties
- **Weyerhaeuser.** The figures span $0.9M to $200M, with no reliable contemporary total. Treat him as unknown, perhaps $50–60M.
- **William Waldorf Astor.** Reputedly the richest American in 1890, but no total fortune was found for 1900–1919. Only "~$60M of NYC real estate" at his death (Time) is sourced. He may belong in the top 10 for 1900–1905.
- **Mellon.** Cannadine's book values ($80M in 1923, $127M peak) and market-value estimates ($300–600M) differ fourfold. R.B. Mellon has a similar spread: $200M reported, $2M in his will, $350M per K&G.
- **Ford.** The 1925 "$1B+" is a press and secondary estimate for a private company. His 1947 estate is stated variously as ">$80M" (probate) and $1B (K&G). Ford Motor was valued at ~$718M in 1943 (Time). Very little value went to heirs as wealth: the non-voting stock went to the Ford Foundation.
- **Rockefeller's peak.** The 1916 "first billionaire" claim and the 1918 $1.2B were contemporary paper valuations. Junior said his father never reached $1B. The $900M 1913 peak is the standard biographical figure.
- **Fortune 1968 table is missing** (p.156, not online). This gap leaves no 1968 figures for Getty, Hughes, Ludwig, H.L. Hunt or the Mellons.
- **Carnegie in 1918** (Forbes: $200M) is wrong. He had already given away most of his eventual $350M of gifts and died with ~$30M. Prefer estate and transfer data.
- **Estates of the 1950s–80s are often unknown.** For example: Ailsa Mellon Bruce 1969, Getty 1976 (only the museum share of $660–700M is known), Hughes 1976 (Time estimated $2.3B; Wikipedia gives a $2.5B split in 1983).
- **Forbes 400 figures** for trusts (Gordon Getty 1984 $4.1B = the Sarah C. Getty Trust) and for families are not individual net worths.
- **Lamon & Charles W. Harkness** both depend on two numbers each ($100M vs $170M; $60M vs $170M) from the same 1915–1918 press.

## Excluded (and why)
- **Monarchs/rulers.** Excluded per the brief: Nizam of Hyderabad, kings, sultans. The Aga Khan III was also excluded as a hereditary religious leader whose figures are unreliable.
- **No sourced figure found:**
  - **W. R. Hearst.** Only unsourced net-worth-site numbers exist; he was deeply indebted by 1937.
  - **Basil Zaharoff.** "One of the richest men", but no credible number.
  - **Simón Patiño.**
  - **Ivar Kreuger.** The 30B SEK figure is "citation needed", and his empire was fraudulent.
  - **August/Fritz Thyssen, Hugo Stinnes.**
  - **Rothschild branches.** No sourced individual totals found; individual estates appear to have been in the low £ millions, far below threshold (not researched in depth).
  - **Indian and Japanese pre-war industrial families** (Tata, Birla, Mitsui, Iwasaki). No credible individual USD figures.
  - **Onassis.** No sourced estate figure found. Niarchos's "$12B at death (1996)" comes from a gossip source and falls after the window.
- **Outside the window:**
  - **Yoshiaki Tsutsumi.** Forbes 1987: $20B, which would be ~1/240 of 1987 GDP if real. His first sourced figure is 1987, so he is excluded. He is the biggest omission if the cut is relaxed to include 1987.
  - **Taikichiro Mori.** Forbes $15B, in 1991.
- **Below threshold:**
  - On the Forbes 1918 list at $50–70M: Schiff, Swift, McCormick, Joseph Widener, Arthur Curtiss James, Nicholas Brady. Guggenheim, Schwab, J.P. Morgan Jr. and Stillman are kept in the files for continuity.
  - Fortune 1957 entries at $200–400M or less, other than those requested: Irénée du Pont, William du Pont Jr., Amy Phipps Guest, A.P. Sloan.
  - Alfred Loewenstein: £12M ≈ $58M at his 1920s peak, about 1/1,700.
  - Harry Payne Whitney: 1930 estate $62.8–77.8M, about 1/1,200.
  - Insull: no figure; he lost everything by 1932.
  - Kettering and Mott: no reliable dated figure.
  - Helen Gould: inherited $10M.
- **Families** (du Pont, Rockefeller cousins, Bass, Cox, Newhouse) are not modelled as single fortunes. Forbes 1986 gives the Newhouse brothers $2B combined and the Cox sisters $2B combined.

## Suggested use for the film
- Interpolate between dated anchors; prefer contemporary lists and estates.
- Use K&G only to cap or order people who otherwise have a single point.
- Show philanthropy as sand leaving the pile in the years given.
- Where only a range exists, the geometric mean is a neutral default, but show the range if possible.
