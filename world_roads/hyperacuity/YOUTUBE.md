Watch full screen at 1080p or higher, and find the pair of lines that's out of line. It starts at 4 pixels and halves every round, down to ⅛ of a pixel.

Most people get further than they expect. You can see a misalignment of just 5 to 10 arcseconds, a third to a sixth of the space between two light sensors (cones) at the centre of your retina. It's called hyperacuity: the optics of your eye blur a line across several cones, and your brain works out its centre from how brightly each one responds. It's the same trick a screen uses to draw a line between pixels.

Comment the last round you got right, and what you watched on.

Eye doctors use this sensitivity too. On an Amsler grid, straight lines that look wavy, bent or missing can be an early sign of a problem in the macula, such as macular degeneration. If straight lines ever look bent to you, see your optometrist. This video is for education and is not a diagnosis.
