# Narration script: The 10 Biggest Single-Game Outliers in Team Sports

Timecodes are positions in the final cut (9:05). Each card is 36 seconds. Never say "proved", "greatest athlete" or
"certain". Every number here is on screen or in facts_a.json.

## Hook (0:00–0:30)

**0:01** Four thousand three hundred and sixty-four season-leading games, in basketball, hockey, baseball and football.

**0:07** Each graded against the best games of everyone else in its era.

**0:11** Most land near zero. Only six reach five.

**0:17** Ten stood clear of the field.

**0:23** These are the ten biggest single-game outliers in team sports, counting down from ten.

## #10 Derrick Thomas, 1990 (0:29)

**0:31** Number ten. Derrick Thomas, Kansas City, against Seattle in 1990: seven sacks in one game.

**0:37** Each dot is one of the five best games by anyone else that season, fifteen years either side, converted to 1990's
sack rate. The line through them is the curve. Each tick above it is one step.

**0:51** A count of seven stands for anything from six and a half to seven and a half, so it's measured from six and a
half. It's still the NFL record.

## #9 Bert Olmstead, 1953–54 (1:05)

**1:06** Nine. Bert Olmstead, Montreal, January 1954: eight points in one game, four goals and four assists.

**1:17** It matched Maurice Richard's record from 1944. Converted to 1954's lower scoring, Richard's eight is about six
and three-quarters.

**1:29** The next best that season was five points.

## #8 Darryl Sittler, 1975–76 (1:40)

**1:42** Eight. Darryl Sittler, Toronto, against Boston in 1976: ten points, six goals and four assists.

**1:52** The curve sits near six points, and one step is under three-quarters of a point. Ten is nearly five steps up.

**2:04** It's still the NHL record.

## #7 Mark Whiten, 1993 (2:16)

**2:17** Seven. Mark Whiten, St. Louis, at Cincinnati in 1993: four home runs and twelve runs batted in.

**2:27** It came in the second game of a doubleheader, and it tied a record set in 1924.

**2:36** The next best that season was eight.

## #6 Bob Cousy, 1958–59 (2:51)

**2:53** Six. Bob Cousy, Boston, February 1959: twenty-eight assists.

**3:01** Guy Rodgers matched it in 1963, when teams passed more. In 1959 terms, that's about twenty-five.

**3:12** Kevin Porter's twenty-nine broke the record in 1978.

## #5 Cloyce Box, 1950 (3:26)

**3:28** Five. Cloyce Box, Detroit, at Baltimore in 1950: three hundred and two receiving yards.

**3:37** The curve is about a hundred and seventy yards, and one step is about twenty-five. Box is more than five steps
up.

**3:48** Football's box scores here start in 1950, so this game is compared only with the seasons after it.

## #4 Jim Bottomley, 1924 (4:02)

**4:03** Four. Jim Bottomley, St. Louis, at Brooklyn in 1924: twelve runs batted in.

**4:12** Six hits, two of them home runs. Most seasons, the best games by anyone else drove in six or seven.

**4:24** Twelve is still the major-league record. Mark Whiten matched it in 1993.

## #3 Norm Van Brocklin, 1951 (4:37)

**4:39** Three. Norm Van Brocklin, Los Angeles, against the New York Yanks in 1951: five hundred and fifty-four passing
yards.

**4:50** Passing has grown since. Converted to 1951's rate, the closest anyone has come is Y.A. Tittle's five hundred and
five in 1962: about four hundred and seventy.

**5:02** It's still the NFL record.

## #2 Kobe Bryant, 2005–06 (5:13)

**5:14** Two. Kobe Bryant, against Toronto in January 2006: eighty-one points, fifty-five of them in the second half.

**5:25** The curve is about fifty-one points, and one step about four and a half. Kobe is nearly seven steps up.

**5:36** Only one player has scored more in an NBA game.

## #1 Wilt Chamberlain, 1961–62 (5:48)

**5:50** Number one. Wilt Chamberlain, Philadelphia, 2 March 1962: one hundred points.

**5:59** The next best that season was sixty-three. Teams averaged almost a hundred and nineteen points a game, and the
score allows for it.

**6:10** It is still almost nine steps above the curve: the biggest single-game outlier in team sports.

## How the score works: grading on a curve (6:23)

**6:25** How does the score work? Picture a class test. Most of the class scores around seventy, give or take about
five points.

**6:33** Call that usual give-or-take one step.

**6:39** A student who scores ninety-five is twenty-five points above the class average: five steps. A score of five.

**6:50** Put the same ninety-five in a class that averages ninety, and it's one step above: a score of one.

**6:59** Same number, different class. So every game is graded against its own era.

## Four rules that keep it fair (7:07)

**7:08** Four rules keep it fair. First, every game is scaled to regulation length: overtime and extra innings are extra
chances.

**7:16** Second, inflation: other seasons' games are converted into the athlete's season, by at most one and a half times.

**7:25** Third, the class is the five best games by anyone else each season, and a count is measured from half a unit
below.

**7:33** And fourth, a game must stand at least one step clear of every other game on its graph. Calvin Johnson's three
hundred and twenty-nine yards come within a step of Jimmy Smith's 2000, so they're left out.
