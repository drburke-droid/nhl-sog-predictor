## How the score works: grading on a curve (6:23)

**6:25** How does the score work? Picture a class test. Most of the class scores around seventy, give or take about
five points.

**6:33** Call that usual give-or-take one step.

**6:39** A student who scores ninety-five is twenty-five points above the class average: five steps. A score of five.

**6:50** Put the same ninety-five in a class that averages ninety, and it's one step above: a score of one.

**6:59** Same number, different class. So every mark is graded against its own era.

## Four rules that keep it fair (7:07)

**7:08** Four rules keep it fair. First, you're graded against your rivals, not yourself.

**7:16** Second, one entry each: an athlete's highest-scoring mark.

**7:24** Third, the supersuit years. In 2008 and 2009 swimmers raced in polyurethane suits, and those two seasons are
treated as a shift.

**7:33** And fourth, a mark must stand at least one step clear of every other mark on its graph. Katie Ledecky's eight
oh four in 2016 isn't: Summer McIntosh's eight oh five, in 2025, comes within a step.
