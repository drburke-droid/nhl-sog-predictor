# Published data on real subjective refraction

For context in the video. Each number was read in the source named: the abstract, or the full text where marked
FT. Numbers marked [secondary] were taken from another paper's summary table and not checked at the original.
Collected by a research pass on 2026-11-26.

## 1. How well two refractions agree (repeatability and reproducibility)

| Study | Design | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Goss & Grosvenor 1996, *J Am Optom Assoc* 67(10):619–630 (review, abstract only) | literature review | Most studies: close to **80% agreement within ±0.25 D** and **95% within ±0.50 D** for spherical equivalent, sphere and cylinder, same or different examiner. |
| Shah, Edgar, Rabbetts, Harle & Evans 2009, *Optom Vis Sci* 86(5):517–528, doi:10.1097/OPX.0b013e31819fa590 (FT) | 3 standardized patients (2 actors) visiting 100–102 community optometrists in England, vs an expert benchmark | Spherical equivalent: **81% within ±0.25 D, 97% within ±0.50 D**; sphere 90% / 98%; cylinder 93% / 100% (the patients had little astigmatism). 95% of the time, any two optometrists' SE differs by **no more than 0.75 D**. |
| MacKenzie 2008, *Ophthalmic Physiol Opt* 28(2):143–150, doi:10.1111/j.1475-1313.2008.00549.x | 40 randomly chosen UK optometrists, one 29-year-old patient | Right-eye SE: mean −0.83 D, SD 0.28 D; 95% limits −1.38 to −0.28 D; 95% reproducibility limit 0.78 D. J0 and J45 limits 0.24 D [secondary]. |
| Bullimore, Fusaro & Adams 1998, *Optom Vis Sci* 75(8):617–622, doi:10.1097/00006324-199808000-00028 | 86 subjects aged 11–60, 2 masked clinicians | SE difference between two optometrists: mean −0.12 D, **95% limits −0.90 to +0.65 D**. The autorefractor's J0/J45 limits were about half the clinicians'. |
| Rosenfield & Chiu 1995, *Optom Vis Sci* 72(8):577–579 | 12 subjects × 5 sessions, one examiner | Same examiner: mean SD ±0.14 D (95% limits ±0.27 D); recommend ±0.50 D as the smallest real change. |
| Otero, Aldaba & Pujol 2019, *J Optom* 12(2):74–83, doi:10.1016/j.optom.2018.09.001 | 50 adults, one clinician | Test-retest within-subject SD: M ±0.13 D, J0 ±0.04 D, J45 ±0.05 D. |
| Taneri et al. 2020, *J Cataract Refract Surg* 46(12):1659–1666, doi:10.1097/j.jcrs.0000000000000343 | retrospective, 1000 eyes, 4 optometrists | Mean absolute SE difference 0.16 ± 0.19 D; axis SD about 10°. |
| Leinonen et al. 2006, *Acta Ophthalmol Scand* 84(4):532–536 | 99 eyes, tests ~45 days apart | SE coefficient of repeatability 0.74 D. |

## 2. How long it takes

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| Carracedo et al. 2018, *Transl Vis Sci Technol* 7(4):11, doi:10.1167/tvst.7.4.11 (FT) | Conventional subjective refraction after retinoscopy: **4:50 ± 1:17 min** (99 participants, 3 optometrists). |
| Otero et al. 2019 (FT) | Conventional refraction **4:37 ± 0:50 min**, not counting the starting point. |
| Kabat et al. 2019, *Clin Ophthalmol* 13:1807–1813, doi:10.2147/OPTH.S213294 (FT) | Manifest refraction 5:38 (3:00–9:00), including autorefraction, cross-cylinder and binocular balance; "roughly 5–10 mins" for an experienced clinician. |
| Venkataraman et al. 2020, *J Clin Med* 9(10):3144, doi:10.3390/jcm9103144 (FT) | Standard Borish-method subjective refraction 9.5 ± 1.6 min. |

## 3. How often glasses come back

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| Freeman & Evans 2010, *Ophthalmic Physiol Opt* 30(1):1–11, doi:10.1111/j.1475-1313.2009.00682.x | 62 of 3091 exams (**1.8%**) were non-tolerance cases; **61% due to the prescription**; sphere the most common error; every corrected Rx within 1.00 D of the original, 84% within ±0.50 D. |
| Hrynchak 2006, *Ophthalmic Physiol Opt* 26(1):111–115, doi:10.1111/j.1475-1313.2005.00351.x | 400 of 25,718 prescriptions (**1.6%**) came back; **59% incorrect refraction**. |
| Beesley, Davey & Elliott 2022, *Ophthalmic Physiol Opt* 42(3):619–632, doi:10.1111/opo.12961 (FT) | Rechecks: 2.3% of exams; 72% refraction error; of prescription-related rechecks, 38% cylinder, 26% over-plus/under-minus, 11% under-plus/over-minus; 86% of recheck Rxs within ±0.50 D of the original. |
| Bist et al. 2021, *Ophthalmic Physiol Opt* 41(3):610–622, doi:10.1111/opo.12796 | Meta-analysis, 5 studies, 205,478 people: **2.1%** (95% CI 1.6–2.7%) non-tolerance, 47% due to refraction error. |

## 4. The textbook sequence

From Wilkinson 2016, *Minus Cylinder Subjective Refraction Techniques for Clinicians*, University of Iowa
EyeRounds.org (a teaching handout, not peer reviewed), pp. 3–6, which follows the standard (Borish) sequence:

1. Objective starting point (retinoscopy or autorefractor).
2. Monocular MPMVA: fog +0.75 D (lose 2–3 lines), reduce 0.25 D at a time, stop when acuity stops improving.
3. Jackson cross-cylinder: axis first if cylinder ≥ 1.00 D, otherwise power first; axis steps narrow 15° → 5°;
   power in 0.50 D with 0.25 D sphere to keep the spherical equivalent; least cylinder giving clearest vision.
4. Second MPMVA if cylinder changed by ≥ 0.50 D or axis by ≥ 10°. Then the other eye.
5. Binocular balance: +0.75 D both eyes (to about 20/30–20/40), dissociate with 3Δ base-up / 3Δ base-down
   prism, add +0.25 D to the clearer eye; endpoint equal, or the dominant eye slightly clearer.
6. Binocular MPMVA: −0.25 D both eyes at a time; no extra minus without an acuity gain.

Not checked at source: Borish's / Benjamin's and Elliott's textbooks themselves; the Shah-table values marked
[secondary]; Goss & Grosvenor beyond its abstract.
