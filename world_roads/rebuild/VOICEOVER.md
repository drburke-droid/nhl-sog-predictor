# Win now, or tear it down? Narration

The video stands on its captions; this is the script for a voice over the top, section by section, timed to the
captions (seconds from the start of each section, `out/timeline.json` gives where each section starts). Every number is
from `../media/rebuild/results.json`.

## hook (20.5 s)
* 1.0  Every franchise in the NHL, NBA, MLB and NFL. Every season for twenty-five years. Brighter is a better finish;
  white is a title.
* 7.0  Some fan bases won't sit through a rebuild.
* 13.4  Does that trap their teams? And does tearing it down actually pay off?

## theory (18 s)
* 1.0  Here's the theory. Rich markets with rabid fans can't stomach a rebuild, so owners trade picks and prospects to
  win now.
* 6.5  And the teams that can tear it down climb higher later, at the cost of lower lows.
* 12.0  We tested it on a hundred and twenty-four franchises and three thousand and twenty-six seasons, with Forbes
  values, every draft pick and every NFL trade.

## stories (20.5 s)
* 1.0  You can find a story for anything. The Yankees and the Dodgers never finished in the bottom three, and won.
* 6.0  The Penguins and the Astros tore it down, and won. The Oilers and the Sixers tore it down, and didn't.
* 13.4  For every Penguins, there's an Oilers. So what do three thousand seasons say?

## richer (20.5 s)
* 3.0  First: do valuable teams avoid the bottom? Yes. The richest third of each league finished bottom three in 6.8
  percent of its seasons; the poorest third, 11.
* 11.0  But the richest teams also finished top three nearly twice as often, and won 47 titles to 18.

## shifted (20.5 s)
* 6.5  And here's the tell: their results spread just as widely as everyone else's. The whole curve just slides toward
  the top.
* 12.4  Compare teams of equal quality, and the "avoid the bottom" effect vanishes: minus 0.29 becomes minus 0.08. Rich
  teams aren't avoiding rebuilds. They're just better.

## winnow (20.5 s)
* 1.0  Do they play win-now? A little. Teams in bigger markets run older rosters, even at equal quality.
* 5.0  Richer teams make slightly fewer of their own first-round picks.
* 9.0  In the NFL, there's no sign that richer teams trade away more first-rounders.
* 12.0  So valuable teams lean win-now, a bit, but it's market size more than fan passion.

## teardown (30.5 s)
* 1.0  Now the big one. Start with a team in the middle of the pack. Eighty-six times, a team like that tore it down;
  a hundred and fifty-nine times, it stayed put.
* 8.2  The tear-downs sank much lower.
* 14.4  And they climbed back... no higher. Their best season came in at the 81st percentile; the teams that stayed put
  hit the 82nd.
* 21.8  Ten years of lower lows, for the same peak.

## titles (25.5 s)
* 0.5  What about championships? Sixteen percent of tear-down teams won a title within ten years, against eleven percent
  of the teams that stayed put: a gap well within chance.
* 6.0  Except in one league: the NBA, where one draft pick can carry a franchise. There, it's 18 percent against 6.
* 15.0  And the champions themselves weren't more likely to have torn down in the decade before their title. Except,
  maybe, in hockey.

## verdict (20.5 s)
* 1.0  So, claim one: rich teams can't stomach a rebuild. Half right. They bottom out less because they're better;
  big markets do run older rosters.
* 6.5  Claim two: tear-downs climb higher. Mostly no. Lower lows, the same peaks, and a better title shot mainly in
  the NBA.
* 13.0  Money buys a better team. A tear-down buys a lottery ticket.

## outro (13 s)
* 0.5  Win now, or tear it down? Be good. The rest is a lottery.
