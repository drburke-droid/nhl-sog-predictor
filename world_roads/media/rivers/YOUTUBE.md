**Description**

```
Divide a river's length by the straight-line distance from its source to its mouth, the story goes, and on average you get π. Einstein explained why rivers meander in 1926, and in 1996 Hans-Henrik Stølum's model river hovered around π. So we traced the 1,000 longest rivers on Earth in OpenStreetMap, source to mouth, and measured them.

🌊 All 1,000 together: 735,919 km of river, 355,789 km in straight lines. Ratio: 2.07, not π. (The median river: 1.98.)
🌊 36 rivers come within 0.2 of π. The nearest of all 1,000 is the Brahmaputra, at 3.16. It gets there by going round the Himalayas, not by meandering.
🌊 The rivers nearest π run low: typically 75% of their course is below 200 m, against 51% for the rest.
🌊 The flatter the land, the closer to π. Rivers falling more than 3 m per km come to 1.90 together; those falling less than 10 cm per km, 2.55.
🌊 Two ways to π: half of the 36 get there by meandering (Siberian tundra, the plains of New South Wales), half by big bends.
🌊 They cluster near the equator and in the Arctic. Flat Arctic tundra rivers come closest of any group, with a typical ratio of 2.7.

The music is built from π: its digits, 3-1-4-1-5-9-2-6…, are the melody, played as steps up a pentatonic scale.
```
