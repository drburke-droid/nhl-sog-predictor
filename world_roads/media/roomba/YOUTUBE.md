**Description**

```
A photon drives at the speed of light with one rule: at every intersection, take any road except the one you came in on. No map, no memory, no plan. Like a Roomba. How long until it has driven every road?

🚗 Calgary, 7,583 km of streets: 1.24 seconds, typically (200 runs). The planned route from my Calgary video does it in 37 milliseconds.
🚗 The contiguous United States, 7.9 million km of road in 44.6 million segments: 2 hours 23 minutes of light, typically (16 runs, from 1 h 33 min to 3 h 48 min).
🚗 Driving each road exactly once would take light 26 seconds. At random, the photon drives the whole network about 330 times over.

The run in the video starts near Lebanon, Kansas, the centre of the lower 48. Half of America's road length is done in under 2 minutes. The last 152 roads, all on Manasota Key, a barrier island in Florida, hold out until 2 h 21 min 17 s. By then the photon has driven 2.54 billion km, farther than Saturn is from the Sun. In 13 of the 16 runs the last road was on a coast. Islands, peninsulas and bayous have few ways in.

What should the photon try next? Tell me in the comments.
```
