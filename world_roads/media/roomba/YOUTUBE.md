**Description**

```
How long would a Roomba take to drive down every road in America? Not clean them, just drive down each one. And so we can watch, it moves at the speed of light.

The first Roombas had no map: drive straight, bump into something, turn a random way, repeat. On roads that becomes: at every intersection, take any road except the one you came in on. At a dead end, turn around.

🧹 New York City, 10,979 km of streets (91,022 blocks): 1.9 seconds, typically (200 runs). The run in the video spends the last 29% of its trip hunting for two blocks in Far Rockaway.
🧹 The contiguous United States, 7.9 million km of road (23 million blocks): 2 hours 23 minutes, typically (16 runs, from 1 h 33 min to 3 h 48 min). The run in the video ends on Beach Road, on Manasota Key, a barrier island in Florida, after 2.54 billion km. That's farther than Saturn is from the Sun.
🧹 At a real Roomba's top speed (0.5 m/s): New York takes 36 years. America takes 163,000 years.

In 13 of the 16 US runs, the last road was on a coast. Islands, peninsulas and bayous have few ways in, so a Roomba with no map can miss them for hours.

What should the Roomba try next? Tell me in the comments.

CHAPTERS

How it's made: road data from Overture Maps Foundation, which includes OpenStreetMap data (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). Motorways down to residential streets, no service roads or tracks; one-way rules ignored. A "block" is a stretch of road between intersections. The US run shown is 14.3 billion simulated junction choices, replayed exactly for the slow-motion ending. Roomba top speed: the 0.5 m/s drive limit of iRobot's Create 2 (Roomba 600 series).
Method, sources and every run: https://github.com/drburke-droid/nhl-sog-predictor/blob/master/world_roads/roomba/ACCURACY.md

Music: original, synthesized for this video.
```
