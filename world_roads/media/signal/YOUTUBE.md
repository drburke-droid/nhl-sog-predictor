**Description**

Before the telegraph, the fastest way to send news across a country was fire: a bonfire on a
mountaintop, seen from the next mountaintop, which lights its own. Aeschylus wrote about it in 458 BC,
the Byzantines used it, the Great Wall had beacon towers along it, England lit its beacons for the Spanish Armada,
and Tolkien gave Gondor a chain of them.

So how many would it take to send a message all the way around the world?

We started in Plymouth and searched real terrain for the fewest mountaintop fires that can each see
the next one over the curve of the Earth. The chain crosses Europe and Asia, jumps the Bering Strait,
and runs across Alaska, arctic Canada and Greenland, where it dies: Iceland is 356 km away, and the two
horizons only reach 304 km. Fire ships close the gap.

* 145 mountain fires + 14 fire ships = 159 relays, 26,512 km
* About 13 hours at 5 minutes per relay
* Longest sightline: 433 km, Mount Foraker to Mount Blackburn (Alaska)
* Every sightline re-checked against terrain every 150 m

Terrain: Terrarium elevation tiles (Mapzen / AWS Open Data). Borders: Natural Earth. Physics: Earth
radius 6,371 km with standard refraction (k = 0.13), 10 m bonfires.

**Description**

Imagine an AI takes over every phone, every satellite, every cable and every radio on Earth. How fast
could we warn every country, using the one network it can't hack?

Fire. A bonfire on a hilltop, seen from the next hilltop, which lights its own. We lit the first one at
Bletchley Park, home of Colossus and of the first AI Safety Summit, and let the warning spread across
real terrain: every fire that can see a burning one lights its own 5 minutes later, so the message
branches out like a wave. No phones, no radio, no boats.

* 176 of 195 capitals warned
* The last one, Buenos Aires, after 14 hours 15 minutes (171 relays)
* Half the world's capitals know within about 4 hours 20 minutes
* 1,326 bonfires, 181,778 km of sightlines
* Africa across the Strait of Sicily, North America across the Bering Strait in one 189 km jump
* Sri Lanka and 18 island nations never get the message: no fire can see that far over the curve of
  the Earth

Total bandwidth: one bit.

Terrain: Terrarium elevation tiles (Mapzen / AWS Open Data); every sightline re-checked against terrain
every 150 m. Borders and capitals: Natural Earth. Physics: Earth radius 6,371 km with standard
refraction (k = 0.13), 10 m bonfires.
