**Description**

Before the telegraph, the fastest way to send news across a country was fire: a bonfire on a
mountaintop, seen from the next mountaintop, which lights its own. Aeschylus wrote about it in 458 BC,
the Byzantines used it, the Great Wall had beacon towers along it, England lit its beacons for the Spanish Armada,
and Tolkien gave Gondor a chain of them.

So how many would it take to send a message all the way around the world?

We started in Plymouth and searched real terrain for the fewest mountaintop fires that can each see
the next one over the curve of the Earth. The chain crosses Europe and Asia, jumps the Bering Strait,
and runs across Alaska, arctic Canada and Greenland, where it dies: Iceland is 356 km away, and the two
horizons only reach 304 km. Fire ships close the gap.

* 145 mountain fires + 14 fire ships = 159 relays, 26,512 km
* About 13 hours at 5 minutes per relay
* Longest sightline: 433 km, Mount Foraker to Mount Blackburn (Alaska)
* Every sightline re-checked against terrain every 150 m

Terrain: Terrarium elevation tiles (Mapzen / AWS Open Data). Borders: Natural Earth. Physics: Earth
radius 6,371 km with standard refraction (k = 0.13), 10 m bonfires.
Method, sources and limits: https://github.com/drburke-droid/nhl-sog-predictor/blob/master/world_roads/ACCURACY.md
