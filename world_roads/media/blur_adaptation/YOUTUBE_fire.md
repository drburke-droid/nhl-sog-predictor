No eye focuses light perfectly. Even with perfect glasses, every point of light lands on your retina as a small, uneven smudge, and the shape of that smudge is unique to you. So why does the world look sharp?

In 2004, scientists at the University of Rochester and the University of Murcia used adaptive optics, the same technology big telescopes use, to cancel people's blur and then put it back: either each person's own smudge, or the exact same smudge turned to a new angle. The amount of blur was identical, but all five people saw the turned blur as blurrier. They had to remove 20 to 40% of it before it looked as clear as their own. Your brain has learned the shape of your blur, and it quietly corrects for it.
