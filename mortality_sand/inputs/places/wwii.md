### Prisoners of war
- **Soviet POWs, 2.95M in six rows by region and period.** Three rows cover the Jul 1941-Apr 1942 mass dying: Belarus and western Russia, Ukraine, and the General Government. A fourth covers Germany. Two more cover 1942-45: one for the occupied USSR, one for the Reich and Poland. This follows Streit, Hartmann and Pohl: ~2M died by early 1942; >2M died in the USSR, ~0.5M in the General Government and ~0.4M in Germany.
- **German POWs.** The Stalingrad captives are one row (~85k, Feb-Jun 1943). German POWs in Soviet camps are another (280k), running 1944-07 to 1947-12. That is allowed because the Germany parent row runs to 1947.

## How deaths were derived from casualties
- **Soviet losses.** I started from Krivosheev's "irrecoverable" losses for each operation: killed, died of wounds, missing and captured. I subtracted prisoners, because Soviet POWs who died are counted at the camps; counting them at the battle as well would double them. For the 1941 encirclements this leaves only ~20-30% of the irrecoverable figure (for example Kyiv: 616k irrecoverable, ~665k claimed captured, ~150k killed). Where Krivosheev is a known undercount I used revisionist estimates: Lopukhovsky for Vyazma, Gerasimova for Rzhev, Glantz for Leningrad.
- **German losses.** These are deaths only, taken as killed plus missing who were not captured. Sources are Frieser (Kursk, Bagration) and Overmans. Austrians are 5% of German military dead.
- **Chinese battle deaths.** Reported "killed and wounded" totals were cut to about 40-45% to get deaths. The Nationalist army's own war total was 1.32M killed against 1.76M wounded, and disease losses were heavy.
- **Republic shares for Soviet soldiers.** These come from Erlikman's military dead by republic (RUS 65%, UKR 16%, BLR 6%, KAZ 3%, UZB 3%, AZE/GEO 2% each...). I shifted them by period:
  - 1941 and the 1941 POWs lean on Ukraine and Belarus.
  - 1942-43 leans on Russia and Central Asia, while Ukraine and Belarus were occupied.
  - From the Dnieper (autumn 1943) on, Ukraine and Belarus are again heavier, because of field conscription in liberated areas.
  - The Caucasus row uses higher Georgian, Azerbaijani and Armenian shares.

## Judgment calls
1. **Nanjing.** Central is 200k (range 40k-300k). The parent row's note says "median ~260,000". I kept 200k, the middle of the serious scholarly range (Kasahara 100-200k, IMTFE 200k+), and take ~35% as captured soldiers from elsewhere.
2. **North China "Three Alls" (1941-42).** Central is 1.2M, low 0.5M, high 2.7M. Himeta's ">2.7M" is the only published figure and includes famine and displacement, so the central is well below it. Confidence is low. This is the largest single number I set by judgment; drop the row if you would rather not localise it.
3. **Soviet POW split by region.** Ukraine 850k, Belarus and western Russia 750k, General Government 450k, Germany 250k for 1941-42, then 450k and 200k for 1942-45. Only the totals for the USSR (>2M), the General Government (~0.5M) and the Reich (~0.4M) are published. The Ukraine/Belarus split is mine, scaled down from national claims (Ukraine ~1.3M, Belarus ~0.8M), which add up to more than the total.
4. **Hungarian Jews at Auschwitz.** I used 340k deaths, not Piper's 430k (which is deportations). About 320k were gassed on arrival, plus later deaths of those selected for work. The deaths are split by today's borders: HUN 38%, ROU 30% (Northern Transylvania), UKR 20% (Subcarpathia), SVK 9%, SRB 3%. This draws the Subcarpathian share from the Holocaust STP (Ukraine) row, as the parent's own split implies.
5. **Leningrad.** Evacuation deaths (~400k) are not placed at the city, because they died elsewhere. Soldiers on the Leningrad fronts get a 70k `LOCAL` share for the city's militia divisions.
6. **East Prussian civilians** are coded `DEU`, not `LOCAL`. `LOCAL` there would draw from today's Russia and Poland, against the wrong parent rows.
7. **Captives dying after a battle.** Stalingrad's ~85k German captives who died are a separate row (Feb-Jun 1943), not part of the battle. Italian and Hungarian captives who died in the first months are inside the Don row (window to Mar 1943).
8. **Windows chosen to match the dying.** Hiroshima and Nagasaki run Aug-Nov 1945, the "2-4 months" over which RERF counts acute deaths. Leningrad's civilians are cut at Dec-Apr and May-Aug, so the monthly peak is right. Chełmno stops at Dec 1942; its small 1944 phase stays in the parent row.
9. **The Yellow River flood** is placed at 500k (Muscolino 400-500k; official 800-900k). See bug 3 below.
10. **The Ustaše row is 220k for 1941-42.** It is the peak period of the 320-340k (USHMM) killed over the war. Jewish victims are excluded.
11. **German POWs in the USSR** are 280k, from Overmans' 363k documented less the Stalingrad captives. His ~1M upper bound is only in `deaths_high`.

## Problems found in `places.py` (not edited, please check)
1. **`POL` means Polynesia in `_home_split`.** It tests `if code in S.VESSELS` before the ISO table, and `POL` is both the Polynesia vessel and Poland's ISO3 code.
   - As written, every `POL` share would draw grains from Polynesia. That covers Polish Jews at Treblinka, Auschwitz, Bełżec, Sobibór, Chełmno and Majdanek (about 1.6M), Polish soldiers in 1939, and Poles at Berlin and Normandy. It would also charge whatever row is nearest.
   - `NAM` has the same clash: North America and Namibia.
   - Fix: check `S.ISO_VESSEL` first, or keep vessel codes out of `from`. My budget figures above assume that fix.
2. **`_pick_row` charges the biggest overlapping row in a region, not the row for that country.**
   - Every EUR place (Polish, Belarusian, French...) is charged to "Germany and Austria" (3.65M, 54%). The Poland, Belarus/Baltic, Yugoslavia and rest-of-Europe rows are untouched.
   - Every STP place is charged to "European Russia", even Ukrainian and Kazakh dead.
   - Manila's 100k and the Burma Railway's Malayan and Javanese labourers are charged to the Dutch East Indies row (2.5M), not the Philippines or Malaya rows.
   - Totals per region are still right, but the leftover spread is skewed: Germany's blob loses Polish and Soviet deaths, while the Poland and Belarus blobs keep theirs. Matching rows by country would fix it.
3. **The Yellow River flood is counted twice in 1938.** The catalogue already has a 1938 flood row (600k), but `_pick_row` charges my flood place to the 12.9M main China row. The flood row then still spreads 600k on top of my 500k. Either drop the catalogue's flood row once this place exists, or have `_pick_row` prefer the nearest overlapping row.

## Possibly missing from the catalogue
- **Winter War, Nov 1939-Mar 1940.** About 127-168k Soviet dead (Krivosheev 126,875). The Soviet WWII rows start in 1941 and the 1930s repression row is not a war, so these deaths have no parent. The same gap covers the Soviet dead in Poland in Sep 1939 (1,475) and Khalkhin Gol 1939 (~10k Soviet, ~9k Japanese).
- **Soviet deportations and executions in eastern Poland and the Baltics, 1939-41** (Katyn and the deportations of 1940-41). They fall under the Poland row (1939-45), but the Belarus/Baltic row starts in 1941.
- **Japanese deaths after 1945.**
  - ~60k Japanese POWs died in Soviet camps 1945-48.
  - ~170k Japanese settlers died in Manchuria in winter 1945-46.
  - The Japan row ends in 1945.
  - The same applies to Hungarian, Romanian and Italian POW deaths in the USSR after 1945.
- **Guangdong famine of 1943.** Often cited at 1-3M. It sits inside the 12.9M China row with no separate place. Whoever covers the war famines could look at it.
