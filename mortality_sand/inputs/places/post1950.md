# Places after 1950: notes

There are 132 rows in `places_post1950.csv`: 79 for COVID-19 and 53 for other post-1950 crises. Every `parent_id` exists in `events_post1950.csv`.

For every parent × country pair, the placed central values fit inside the catalogue's central value; the table at the end gives the numbers. The film's own WPP-based budget is another matter: many non-COVID places exceed it (see "Conflict with the film's WPP budget").

The build script, the checks and the downloaded data are in `scratchpad/places/`:
- `build_places.py` writes the CSV;
- `check.py` runs the checks;
- `econ_monthly.csv`, `ru_excess_region_month.csv`, `br_excess_month_2019base.csv` and `eu_weekly_excess.csv` are intermediate data.

## Coverage

### COVID-19 (`covid19`): 79 rows, about 4.85M deaths placed
Rows are excess deaths from all-cause registration data wherever such data exist. They are never reported COVID deaths.

| Country | Rows | Data and method |
|---|---|---|
| USA | 12 | CDC NCHS weekly excess by state (dataset xkkf-xrst): observed minus average expected, summed over each wave's months. Waves: NYC spring 2020; NJ & NY suburbs; Texas and Florida summer 2020; Upper Midwest & Dakotas and the Great Lakes, autumn 2020; California and Texas winter 2020-21; Delta in Florida, Texas, the Deep South and TN/KY/Carolinas. |
| India | 12 | CRS registered deaths by state, 2021 (and 2020) minus 2019 × population growth. Where 2019 registration was incomplete (UP 63%, Bihar 52%, MP 89%), the central is the midpoint of the registered increase and the completeness-scaled increase. 70-85% of each state's 2021 excess goes to the Delta months; Maharashtra's window is Mar-Jun. Also: Delhi (civic-body registrations), Maharashtra first wave, and South India (AP, Karnataka, TN) first wave, Jul-Oct 2020. |
| Brazil | 8 | Civil-registry (ARPEN) monthly registrations by state and city, against the same month of 2019 × 1.015 (2020) or × 1.03 (2021). Earlier years are incompletely scraped, so a trend baseline fails. Rows: Manaus ×2, São Paulo ×2, Rio ×2, Minas Gerais and the South at the Gamma peak. |
| Russia | 12 | Rosstat deaths by region and month of death (Kobak's repo), against a 2015-19 linear trend per month. Moscow ×3, St Petersburg Delta, and four macro-regions × the two autumn waves: Central (excl. Moscow), Volga, South + N. Caucasus, Urals + Siberia. |
| South Africa | 5 | SAMRC cumulative excess natural deaths by province, differenced between report dates: 29 Dec 2020, 1 May 2021, 13 Nov 2021. Rows: Eastern Cape waves 1-2, KZN Beta, KZN Delta, Gauteng Delta, Western Cape waves 1-2. |
| Europe | 5 | Eurostat weekly deaths by NUTS region, against the 2016-19 weekly mean: Bergamo, rest of Lombardy, Madrid, Catalonia, London. |
| Latin America | 9 | Mexico City + México State ×2, Lima & Callao ×2, Guayaquil, Bogotá, Buenos Aires ×2, Santiago. Sources are national or city reports; see each row. |
| China | 13 | Wuhan, Jan-Mar 2020 (Liu et al. BMJ 2021: 5,954). The exit wave of Dec 2022-Jan 2023 for the 12 provinces with the most deaths, from Xiao et al. 2023 (JAMA Netw Open). |
| Others | 3 | Istanbul (low confidence), Jakarta (burials), Java Delta (low confidence). |

**China's exit wave.** National estimates for Dec 2022-Jan 2023:
- Xiao et al. 1.87M (95% CI 0.71-4.43M; age 30+, all provinces but Tibet);
- a household survey (31 provinces) about 1.35M;
- Du et al. (EID 2023, modelled, Dec-Feb) about 1.41M;
- the urban-only obituary estimate (Epidemiology 2024) about 0.92M.

Peak timing ran north to south and city to countryside. Beijing peaked about 15-25 Dec. Shanghai, Guangdong and Chongqing peaked in late Dec to early Jan. Henan, Sichuan and rural areas peaked in January, around the Spring Festival (22 Jan).

The 12 province rows hold 1.25M. I left out Heilongjiang (73k), Anhui (63k), Shaanxi (48k), Yunnan (47k), Shanxi (43k), Chongqing (41k) and the rest. The reason: if the film spreads a two-month row evenly, half falls in December 2022, and the film's CHN budget for all of 2022 is only 632k.

### Other crises: 53 rows
- **Great Leap Forward famine:** 11 rows. Cao Shuji's county-gazetteer provincial tolls (32.46M total) are scaled ×0.924 to the catalogue's 30M. Low and high are scaled to Peng's 23M and Yang's 36M. Xinyang (about 1M, Oct 1959-Apr 1960) is split out of Henan. The ten provinces plus Xinyang hold 26.8M, which leaves 3.2M for the provinces not listed. Years only (1959-1961), except Xinyang.
- **Ethiopia 1983-85:** Wollo, Tigray, and the resettlement sites as a converge row (MSF ~100k, de Waal ~50k).
- **Tigray War:** Tigray civilians (Ghent).
- **Biafra:** the 1968 famine and the 1969 shrinking enclave.
- **DR Congo:** the Kivus, Ituri/Orientale, and northern Katanga with Maniema. The east's share (~85%) follows from the IRC east-west mortality rates; the split within the east is mine.
- **Sudan:** Darfur 1984-85 (de Waal), Darfur 2003-05 and the camps 2005-08 (Degomme & Guha-Sapir), and the Bahr el Ghazal famines of 1988 and 1998.
- **Syria:** OHCHR 2022 estimated civilian deaths by governorate for Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Deir ez-Zor, Idlib and Homs. These are civilians only; combatants stay in the national row.
- **Iraq:** Baghdad 2004-08, and the Anfal in Iraqi Kurdistan.
- **Iran-Iraq War:** two converge rows (southern front; central and northern front), from IRN:0.6;IRQ:0.4.
- **Russia-Ukraine 2022-23:**
  - Mariupol (local);
  - Bakhmut (converge RUS:0.73;UKR:0.27);
  - the Russian front line (converge RUS);
  - the Ukrainian front line (converge UKR).

  Russian placements total 119.9k, against the catalogue's 120k.
- **Bangladesh 1971:** refugee camps in India, as a converge row from BGD (Jalal 2025).
- **Indonesia 1965-66:** East Java, Central Java, Bali, and North Sumatra & Aceh.
- **Cultural Revolution:** Guangxi 1967-68.
- **Chechen wars:** two converge rows (LOCAL plus Russian soldiers).
- **Boko Haram:** Borno and the north-east (UNDP).
- **Point disasters:** each parent is placed whole at its place. Tangshan, Banqiao (Zhumadian), Aceh, the Bhola coast, the Chittagong coast (1991) and the Irrawaddy delta (Nargis). These were not on the brief's list. I added them because no events are more tied to one place and one month, and they are high-confidence; drop them if unwanted.

## Judgment calls
1. **No country-wide rows for Poland, Czechia, Romania or Ukraine.** The film already has each country's monthly timing from The Economist. Eurostat NUTS-2 data show these waves were spread almost evenly inside each country:
   - Polish voivodeships, autumn 2020: 1.5-2.0 excess deaths per 1,000.
   - Romanian regions, autumn 2021: 1.9-2.7 per 1,000.

   A row centred on the whole country would add nothing. If you want them anyway (Eurostat, my baseline):

   | Country | Wave | Excess deaths |
   |---|---|---|
   | Poland | Oct-Dec 2020 | 65.5k |
   | Poland | Mar-Apr 2021 | 39.8k |
   | Poland | Oct-Dec 2021 | 47.8k |
   | Czechia | Oct 2020-Mar 2021 | 33.5k |
   | Czechia | autumn 2021 | 8.6k |
   | Romania | Oct-Dec 2020 | 28.7k |
   | Romania | Sep-Nov 2021 | 44.0k |
   | Bulgaria | Oct-Dec 2021 | 19.8k |
   | Ukraine (Economist) | Oct-Dec 2020 | 43k |
   | Ukraine (Economist) | Sep-Dec 2021 | 90k |

   No regional series for Ukraine was found. Poland's autumn-2020 Eurostat excess (65.5k) is larger than WPP's figure for all of Poland in 2020 (61k).
2. **Requested but not given a row.**
   - Tehran: Ghafari et al. give 8.8k to Sep 2020, about 15% of Iran's toll and in line with its population. No later provincial split was found, and Iran's waves were national.
   - Egypt, Pakistan and the Philippines: no sub-national excess series was found.
   - Arizona: its summer wave (6.6k) and winter wave (9.6k) are below the visibility threshold.
   - Mosul (9k), St Petersburg autumn 2020 (11.5k) and Paris spring 2020 (11.5k): computed but dropped as small, to keep the row count down.
   - HIV/AIDS by South African province: not easy. Thembisa's provincial outputs are not readily downloadable.
   - Vietnam, by region: there is no sourced regional split.
   - Korean War: skipped, as the brief allowed.
3. **Small but kept:**
   - Iconic first outbreaks: Wuhan, Bergamo, Manaus ×2, Guayaquil.
   - Explicitly requested places: London 10k, Santiago 9.5k, Istanbul 15k, Bogotá 15k, Western Cape 16k, KZN Delta 17.5k, Florida summer 17.8k, Upper Midwest 17.6k.
4. **Allocations that are mine (low confidence).** No published split exists for these:
   - the split of states into wave months (India);
   - Edomex scaled from CDMX;
   - Lima's second-wave share;
   - Bogotá's wave share;
   - Buenos Aires wave split from P-scores;
   - Java apportioned from Indonesia's official COVID deaths;
   - Wollo/Tigray;
   - Biafra 1968 vs 1969;
   - DRC provinces;
   - Indonesia 1965 provinces;
   - the Iran-Iraq front split;
   - Ukrainian deaths at Bakhmut;
   - Baghdad's share of Hagopian's excess.

   The notes column of each row says how it was derived.
5. **Refugees and Bangladesh 1971.** Jalal's 563k (95% CI 324-803k) is the only systematic estimate. I set the central to 400k because it is a single modelled study and would otherwise be more than half the catalogue's 1M.
6. **Tangshan and Banqiao centrals follow the catalogue.** Tangshan uses 300k, not the official 242k, and Banqiao 100k, so that the whole parent sits at the place.
7. **Russian soldiers' home regions.** Converge `from` takes only ISO3, so Russian soldiers are RUS:1.0. Mediazona shows deaths concentrated in Bashkortostan, Buryatia, Tuva, Krasnodar, Sverdlovsk and Chelyabinsk. If the film splits RUS into STP and SIB, a higher SIB share than population would be closer to the truth.
8. **Radii.** A few vast regions use radii above 400 km: Siberia + Urals 800, Upper Midwest 450, Southern Brazil 450, Volga 500, Uttar Pradesh 400, South India 450.

## Conflict with the film's WPP budget (please read)
I checked each row against two budgets, spreading every row evenly over its months:
1. The catalogue rows: always within.
2. The film's per-country-year allocation (`post1950_lifts` logic on `data/wpp_excess.csv`).

**COVID.** Every row fits the film's WPP budget except **Lima's first wave**. WPP 2024 gives Peru only 39k excess deaths in 2020; SINADEF and The Economist give about 100k. The Lima row (45k in May-Sep 2020) is 6k over.

**Other crises.** WPP smooths most crises in countries with poor registration, so the film budget is often far below the catalogue:

| Crisis | Film budget | Catalogue central | Places |
|---|---|---|---|
| Second Congo War | 96k | 3.0M | 2.4M |
| Biafra | 243k | 1.0M | 0.8M |
| Great Leap famine | 15.0M | 30M | 26.8M; ~11.8M over the film's 1959 and 1961 budgets |
| Tangshan | 0 | 300k | 300k |
| Banqiao | 0 | 100k | 100k |
| Darfur | 123k | 300k | 280k |
| Iraq 2003- | 118k | 500k | 150k |
| Iran-Iraq War (Iraq share) | 70k | 250k | 188k |

Bhola, Boko Haram, Chechnya, Anfal and the Bahr el Ghazal 1988 famine are also over in some years.

If the film caps places at its budget, these places will shrink or vanish. Two fixes are possible: let catalogue rows override WPP for named crises, or scale the places to the budget. I kept the scholarly numbers; the table at the end gives the overflow per parent.
