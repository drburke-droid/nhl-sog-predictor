# Audit: battle, siege, sack and campaign rows of wars.csv (non-WWI)

`audit_wars.csv` holds 87 audited rows in the header of the places CSVs plus a first column `old_place`. Rows are matched on `old_place`; three place names occur twice in wars.csv under different parents (Kaifeng: mongol_conquests and ming_qing_transition; Sichuan: the same two; Baghdad: mongol_conquests and timur), so match on (`parent_id`, `old_place`). No row is deleted and no row is added. Built by `audit_tools/build_audit.py` from `audit_tools/audit_data.py`, which also re-runs the parent-capacity check.

## Biggest changes

- **Circassia: final conquest** (caucasus_conquest): 150,000 -> 300,000 (+100%). Old 150k sat below Richmond's own conservative components; with the ports row the expulsion now totals ~500k, between Bullough (333-400k) and Richmond (625k+).
- **Russia 1812: retreat & Berezina** (french_rev_napoleonic_wars): 300,000 -> 410,000 (+37%). Old 300k left the 1812 total at ~525k (GA ~380k, Russian army ~130k, no civilians). Bogdanovich's archival 210k Russian dead and the 65.5k recorded POW deaths raise it.
- **Judea: Bar Kokhba revolt** (jewish_roman_wars): 250,000 -> 350,000 (+40%). Old 250k assumed Dio inflated twofold; the latest dedicated study supports Dio, so central moved toward it.
- **Spain: Peninsular War** (french_rev_napoleonic_wars): 450,000 -> 545,000 (+21%). Old 450k implied only ~265k Spanish dead; Esdaile's 300-500k and Prados de la Escosura's ~0.5M put Spanish deaths near 400k (350k outside Zaragoza).
- **Jerusalem** (jewish_roman_wars): 150,000 -> 75,000 (-50%). Old 150k exceeded the whole besieged population implied by modern estimates of the city (60-80k) plus refugees; old high used Tacitus's 600k.
- **Baghdad** (mongol_conquests): 100,000 -> 150,000 (+50%). Old 100k sat below both the conqueror's boast (200k) and al-Dhahabi's 'most truthful' 300k; new central 150k, high 300k.
- **Yangzhou** (ming_qing_transition): 80,000 -> 100,000 (+25%). Old 80k cited unnamed 'recent estimates'; rebuilt from Struve's 300k population ceiling.
- **Acre siege** (crusades): 50,000 -> 35,000 (-30%). Old 50k had no component build; Hosler's 19k crusader dead is the best count.

8 rows changed by more than 20%; 15 rows changed central at all; the other 72 were verified and kept (some with new ranges, `from`, labels or sources).

Net effect on the audited rows: 54,106,920 -> 54,531,920 central deaths (+0.8%).

## Method

- Deaths only, built by side: each army, plus civilians, with a central value, a range and a named source; the components column shows the arithmetic. Casualty figures (killed + wounded + captured) were converted by taking killed outright plus wounded who died, never surviving prisoners or wounded.
- Pre-modern sacks: chronicle figures (Merv 0.7-1.3M, Nishapur 1.75M, Herat 1.6-2.4M, Urgench 1.2M, Baghdad 0.8-2M, Yangzhou 800k, Jerusalem 1.1M) are never the central value. Centrals are built from plausible city populations (Chandler, Struve/Zhang Defang, Broshi/Levine, Xu Xin) plus refugees; Morgan (1986 pp.74-77), Atwood (2004 p.344) and May (2018 p.63) are the authority that the Khorasan cities 'could only have supported fractions of their reported populations'. `deaths_high` is a plausible maximum; the chronicle number stays in the notes.
- Napoleonic and 19th-century wars: Clodfelter, Bodart, Bogdanovich, Zamoyski, Mikaberidze (POW deaths), Esdaile, Prados de la Escosura; Hacker for the US Civil War; Whigham & Potthast vs Kleinpenning/Reber for Paraguay; Hall and Erickson for the Balkans.
- Census-drop rows (Taiping provinces, Dungan, Mongol North China and Sichuan, Ming-Qing Shaanxi/Henan/Sichuan, An Lushan, Late Han, Thirty Years' War regions, Commonwealth): kept as shares of the parent's deaths, never as the raw population loss; each such row's notes carry the label 'POPULATION-LOSS SHARE, NOT A COUNT' (or 'population-loss based, not a count'). The Taiping provinces hold 16.1M of the parent's 20M (deaths ~25-30% of each province's population loss); that is not overstated against 20-30M deaths and Li & Lin's 47-95M net population loss.
- Sources were fetched (Wikipedia pages with their cited works, necrometrics.com, PMC, journal abstracts, Portuguese and Chinese press); rows I could not re-verify online are kept with confidence `low` and say so.

### Parent-row problems
- **wars_three_kingdoms_britain (EUR):** the four rows now hold 550k of the 600k central (92%, just under the 95% cap), and their highs sum to 882k against a parent high of 870k (England's high is Britannica's 85k combat + 127k non-combat = 212k). The parent's central looks about right; its high is slightly low.
- **caucasus_conquest (STP):** the two Circassian rows now hold 500k of 800k. The parent must also cover 1817-1863 (Chechnya, Dagestan, Russian soldiers), so with Richmond's minimum of 625k for the Circassians alone the parent central looks low, and it ends in 1864, so exile deaths in 1865 cannot be placed.
- **us_civil_war (NAM):** no overflow (503k of 700k), but the parent central of 700k sits below Hacker's census-based 750k, which it cites; about 56k prison deaths outside Andersonville stay in the parent.
- **dungan_revolt (EAS):** unchanged at 92% of the parent; close to the cap. The parent (8M) is itself a census-loss figure scaled down; the rows' lows (300k each) show how weak it is.
- **jewish_roman_wars (LEV):** 495k of 600k (83%) after Jerusalem down and Bar Kokhba up.
- No other parent goes above 95% of its central; no low or central overflows.
- Known gaps carried over: Spanish soldiers in Cuba (no EUR `cuban_wars` row), Moroccan Regulares at Teruel/Ebro (no NAF `spanish_civil_war` row), Circassian exile deaths in 1865.

### Overlap checks
- Zaragoza is excluded from Spain; Massena's Portugal is separate from Spain; Borodino is excluded from the 1812 advance; the 1812 prisoners who died (Mikaberidze's 65.5k by Feb 1813) are counted once, in the retreat row (there is no Napoleonic POW-camp row).
- US theater rows exclude prison deaths, so Andersonville (Georgia, inside the Western Theater radius) is not double counted.
- Mongol Kaifeng (1232-33) is excluded from 'North China: Jin collapse'; Ming Kaifeng (1642) is excluded from Henan; Merv, Nishapur and Herat are excluded from Khorasan; Nanjing (Jul 1864) is now explicitly excluded from Southern Jiangsu.
- Baghdad 1258 (Mongols) and Baghdad 1401 (Timur) are different events; Jerusalem 70, Galilee 67-69 and Bar Kokhba 132-136 do not overlap; Circassia (to May 1864) and the Ottoman ports (from Mar 1864) cover different people (those still in the Caucasus vs those shipped).
