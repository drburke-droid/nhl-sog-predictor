# YouTube listing: 2,000 years of catastrophe

Wording rule: whenever the listing speaks of family, it means your **direct** family line. The claim is that every one
of your direct ancestors lived long enough to have a child. It is not that your family was spared: many relatives
died in these catastrophes, and so did some direct ancestors, after their children were born.

## Description

```
Each grain of sand is 5,000 people. Whenever more people die than normal, their grains are lifted away: 640 million lives across 2,000 years of plagues, wars and famines.
Through all of it, every one of your direct ancestors lived long enough to have a child. Your direct family line was never broken.

Not everyone in your family tree was so lucky. Brothers and sisters, cousins, whole branches, and even some of your direct ancestors after their children were born, are among the grains lifted away here. Many of these catastrophes took the young and the weak first. Yet in every generation before you, someone in your direct line grew up, had a child, and passed the torch on. If a single one had not, you would not be here.

WHAT YOU ARE SEEING
• The map is a table of glass vessels filled with sand. The sand is the world's people, 1 grain for every 5,000. A few crowded regions are packed tighter to fit their glass, as noted on screen.
• Lifted grains are deaths above normal, meaning above each place's usual death rate at the time. They are coloured by cause: red for war, conquest and mass killing; green for epidemics; orange for famine; blue for earthquakes, floods and storms; lavender for other years above normal (after 1950).
• Soldiers' grains leave their home countries and travel to the fronts where they died.
• Bottom left: the deadliest catastrophes so far, ranked as they happen.
• Before 1950 the film follows 123 catastrophes with published death tolls, so only events large enough to be counted are included. From 1950 it uses the United Nations' death counts for every country, every year. Every number is an estimate, and many have wide ranges.
```
