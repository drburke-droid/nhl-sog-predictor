Reinforcement-learning experiment · 5 skaters + goalie a side · self-play

Did the agents invent hockey positions?

Two teams of learning agents played each other on a simulated rink, with only the rules of hockey hard-coded. Nobody told them what a defenceman or a winger is. Loading results…

Watch the trained agents play

Overhead view, one dot per player. Blue attacks to the right. Numbers are slot ids, the only identity an agent has. Every slot runs the same network; the slot number is one of its inputs. After every goal each skater is dropped at a random spot in his own half, so any structure you see they rebuild themselves.

The answer

Where each slot spends its time

Each rink is drawn from that slot's team's point of view, attacking to the right, pooled over both teams and every evaluation game. Darker means more time spent there.

Which real position does each slot match?

Every frame, the five skaters are labelled by where they stand: the two deepest are defence (the one further left is LD), and the other three are forwards, left to right LW, C, RW. The table shows how often each slot gets each label. A slot with a real position lands in one column almost every frame. With no positions every slot would sit near 20% everywhere.

Emergence

How the positions formed during training

Role consistency is the share of frames in which each slot holds its own position, under the best slot-to-position match. The defence/forward line asks only whether the same two slots are the deepest. The dashed lines show what positionless play would score.

The bench

When the coach pulls the goalie

The coach decides once per second of game time whether the goalie should be on the bench. It is the only agent rewarded for winning: +1 for a win, 0 for a tie, −1 for a loss. The grid shows the chance it picks goalie out, by score and time left, with its team carrying the puck in the offensive zone.
